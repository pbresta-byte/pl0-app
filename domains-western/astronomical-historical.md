## Ancient Cosmology (Aristotle)
Aristotle establishes the geocentric model that underpins both Western and
Vedic astronomy: celestial spheres, the unmoved mover, sublunary vs superlunary
realms. His cosmology is structurally identical to Vedic bhuvana descriptions.

## Ibn Ezra's Bridge
Ibn Ezra (1140 CE) bridges Arabic, Hebrew and Latin astrological traditions.
His introductions cover: zodiac signs, planetary natures, houses, aspects,
and elections — providing the transmission route from Hellenistic to medieval
European astrology.

## The Gospel in the Stars (Rolleston & Bullinger)
Both authors argue the 12 constellations encode a prophetic narrative:
Virgo (the seed) through Leo (the lion triumphant). Each decan adds detail.
This parallels the Jyotish nakshatra system where each lunar mansion has
a deity and a symbolic story.

## Cross-tradition notes
- Aristotle's spheres ≈ Jyotish bhuvana lokas (planes of existence)
- Rolleston's Mazzaroth narrative ≈ Jyotish nakshatra purusha stories
- Bullinger's star names ≈ Jyotish nakshatra star names (both trace to ancient Semitic sources)
- Ibn Ezra's system shows the shared Hellenistic root of Western tropical and Indian sidereal
